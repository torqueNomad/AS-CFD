01 · Summary

Results at 6 knots

The shell is a 7.33 m × 1.48 m × 0.90 m pod. Its resistance depends strongly on direction: heave (motion through the broad top face) costs about 60 times more than surge at the same speed.

{{TILES}}
  • Surge (+Z, rounded end first) is friction-dominated: about 70 % of the drag is skin friction, and the flow stays attached to the stern. At 6 kn: {{D6_surge}} N, {{P6_surge}} kW.
  • Sway (+X) and heave (+Y) are bluff-body flows: 95–99 % of the drag is pressure drag from a wide separated wake. At 6 kn: sway {{D6_sway}} N ({{P6_sway}} kW), heave {{D6_heave}} N ({{P6_heave}} kW).
  • Hydraulic power is drag × speed, the power delivered to the water. Thruster shaft or electrical power is higher: divide by the propulsive and motor efficiencies (typically 0.3–0.6 overall for small thrusters).
  • Values include the mesh correction from the medium-mesh runs ({{CORR}}).

03 · Flow visualisation

What the water does

Speed is shown as |U|/V, the local speed divided by the vehicle speed: 1.0 is undisturbed water, values above 1 are flow accelerating around the body, values near 0 are the stagnation point and the wake. Pressure is shown as Cp = p / (½ρV²): +1 at the stagnation point, negative where the flow accelerates.

04 · Verification

Mesh sensitivity, convergence and a friction check

Mesh sensitivity at 6 kn

Three mesh levels with the same refinement topology and background cells of 0.64, 0.48 and 0.36 m (body surface cells about 20, 15 and 11 mm, three prism layers). Surge on coarse, medium and fine; sway and heave on coarse and medium. Speed sweeps were run on the coarse mesh and scaled by the medium/coarse ratio of each axis.

Iterative convergence

Surge runs reached the residual targets (p 1e-5; U, k, ω 1e-6) in 520–620 iterations, with a drag oscillation below 0.1 %. Sway and heave never settle completely, because the separated wake is unsteady and a steady solver can only approximate it. They were run for 1300 iterations, and drag is averaged over the last 30 %. The 1σ oscillation is 1–3 %, but in heave at 4 kn the mean drifted by up to 8 % during the run. That drift is the main reason heave uses a constant-Cd fit.

05 · Wall resolution

y+ on the body

y+ is the non-dimensional height of the first cell centre above the wall. Wall functions are designed for 30 < y+ < 300. The Spalding wall function used here (nutUSpaldingWallFunction) follows one continuous law of the wall, so it also remains valid in the buffer layer (5–30). Values are area-weighted on the coarse mesh; the medium mesh has cells 25 % smaller and correspondingly lower y+.

06 · Method

Geometry, mesh and solver set-up

Geometry sealing

The supplied STL is an assembly of 294 thin panels (2 mm skin) with open ends, ~300 mm side cut-outs near both ends, and through-holes. As instructed, all openings were treated as closed (blind). The body was sliced every 10 mm along Z, and the outer skin radius was taken in 240 angular bins per slice. Bins with no skin (2.3 % of the surface) were interpolated from neighbouring slices, and both ends were capped flat. The result is a watertight envelope: 7.23 m³, wetted area 27.56 m², matching the CAD bounding box within 1 mm.

Domain, mesh and physics

Body axesZ = surge, X = sway, Y = heave (CAD frame). Motion in +Z, +X, +Y; free stream U = −V·ê in the body frame.
DomainBox: 1.5 L or 8 m upstream (whichever larger), 3 L or 15 m downstream, 6 m beyond the body laterally. Blockage 0.6 % (surge), 2.3 % (sway), 3.7 % (heave).
MeshsnappyHexMesh, hex-dominant. Body surface refinement level 5, feature edges level 6, near-body box level 2, wake box level 3, 3 prism layers (expansion 1.2). Surge cells: {{CELLS_C}} (coarse), {{CELLS_M}} (medium), {{CELLS_F}} (fine). checkMesh "Mesh OK" for every mesh; layer coverage above 98.9 %.
SolverOpenFOAM v2512 simpleFoam (steady, incompressible, SIMPLEC). k-ω SST turbulence model. Second-order linear-upwind convection for U, first-order upwind for k and ω.
Boundary conditionsInlet: fixed velocity, turbulence intensity 1 %, eddy-viscosity ratio 10. Outlet: p = 0, inletOutlet. Sides: slip. Body: no-slip, nutUSpaldingWallFunction, kqRWallFunction, omegaWallFunction.
FluidSea water 15 °C, 35 g/kg: ρ = 1025.9 kg/m³, ν = 1.1883×10⁻⁶ m²/s (ITTC Recommended Procedure 7.5-02-01-03, 2011).
ForcesPressure + viscous force on the body, every iteration (forces function object). Drag = force component along the free stream, averaged over the last 30 % of iterations.
Compute4 CPU cores, 4 MPI ranks. Coarse runs 10–30 min, medium about 1 h each.
07 · Assumptions

Assumptions and their effect on the result

#AssumptionWhy, and effect on the numbers
A1All openings, holes and both open ends are closed (blind), flush with the surrounding skin; ends capped flat.Your instruction. Real open holes add internal-flow and cavity drag, which is not captured.
A2The outer surface between known skin is interpolated (2.3 % of the area), including the ~300 mm side cut-outs.The CAD has no skin there; a smooth bridge is the neutral choice.
A3Features below about 20 mm (panel joints, fasteners) are smoothed out.Below mesh resolution. Such features usually add a few percent of drag.
A4Fully submerged and deep, far from the free surface, seabed and walls.Your instruction. No wave-making drag. Valid for depth greater than about 3× body height.
A5Standard sea water at 15 °C, 35 g/kg (ρ 1025.9 kg/m³, ν 1.1883×10⁻⁶ m²/s, ITTC 7.5-02-01-03).Your instruction. Drag scales with ρ; the friction part changes about 2–3 % per 5 °C.
A6Steady speed, pure translation along one axis at a time.No combined motion, drift angle or acceleration; added mass is not included.
A7Motion directions +Z, +X, +Y; the rounded high-Z end is the bow.Sway and heave are close to symmetric. Surge in reverse (blunt flat stern first) would give more drag.
A8Steady RANS, k-ω SST with wall functions.Industry standard at Re 10⁵–10⁷. Typical accuracy ±5–10 % for attached flow and ±15–30 % for separated bluff-body flow.
A9Fully turbulent boundary layer from the leading edge.No transition model. It may over-predict friction slightly at 1 kn.
A10Hydraulically smooth surface, no fouling.Fouling can add 10–50 % or more to friction drag. Add a margin for service conditions.
A11Inflow turbulence intensity 1 %, eddy-viscosity ratio 10.Calm, tow-tank-like water; small effect on drag.
A12Sway and heave wakes are unsteady; reported drag is the time average of the steady solver's iterations.Captures the mean drag only approximately. Shedding forces need URANS or DES.
A13Hydraulic power = drag × speed.Power delivered to the water. Shaft or electrical power = P ÷ (propulsive × motor efficiency).
A14Intermediate speeds come from a Cd fit through the 1, 2, 4 and 6 kn runs.Cd varies smoothly and weakly with Re at these Re. Heave uses a constant mean Cd.
A15Speed sweeps on the coarse mesh, scaled by each axis's medium-mesh correction at 6 kn.Limited by the 4-core machine. The mesh spread is reported as discretisation uncertainty.
A16Domain blockage 0.6–3.7 % with slip side walls, uncorrected.May over-predict sway and heave drag by a few percent.
A17Body only: no thrusters, appendages, tether or interaction effects.Not present in the supplied CAD.
